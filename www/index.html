        Dent shape
        
            
          
        
          
          
          
        
            The notch a pierce cuts into this layer, in its own pixels, at the
            End Point. Depth is how far the point drives in along the approach;
            Width is how wide the opening is across the surface. Both grow from
            nothing at the Enter Point, and shrink back to nothing on the way
            out. Set Depth or Width to 0 for no notch.
          
        
        
        

        
        
          
        
            
        Depths
        
            
          
        
          
        
          
        
        

        
        
        
        
          A testing aid, right on the artwork: tips pink, pierceable cyan,
          the part that bunches up around a dent amber, and walls white — plus
          the live contact reading. Leave it off for normal work.
        
        
        

        
        
          
        
        
      
        
    
        

    
        
      
        
        
        Enter, Dent & End Points
        
        
        
          In scene pixels, measured from this piercer's painted tip to the nearest
          pierceable pixel. Enter is the gap where contact starts;
          End is how much deeper the push keeps growing before it stops.
        
        
        
        
          
        Dent Trigger Distance
        
          
        
        
        
        
          A separate gap: where the notch starts to appear. Contact,
          z-order and force all begin at the Enter Point; the dent begins here
          and is complete at the End Point. Set it closer than Enter for a tip
          that touches first and only dents once it is further in.
        
        
        
        
          
          
          
        
        
        
        
        
          
          
        
            Drag the three handles along the needle's path: Enter where
            contact begins, Dent where the notch starts to appear, and
            End where both stop growing.

        Tip: works best with Px Pin
        
        
        
          Pin the piercer's tip pixels with Px Pin before you use it. Pinned
          pixels are held exactly at their rest position and are immune to bone
          rotation and spring physics, so the tip stays precise instead of
          jiggling — which gives the most accurate, stable contact point.
